| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |

Rules (fixed in advance, not changed after the result): everybody has a cap on the number of chores, and nobody is given a chore they cannot do. Within those limits the script takes the split with the smallest weekly total cost. Then payments that add up to zero make sure that nobody, by their own costs, would rather have another person's share (with the payment that goes with it), and among such payments the worst-off person is as well off as possible.

Total weekly cost (each person's own costs): 135.00. After the compensation everybody comes to -45.00 a week.

The people who pay put the money into a common pot and the people who receive take theirs from it: 25.00 is paid in and 25.00 is taken out, which is exactly equal, so nobody outside has to pay.

After rounding to the cent nobody envies another share (largest envy 0.00). The payments are rounded down or up to a cent and add up to exactly 0.00.

Tie rule: more than one split can have the smallest total cost. The chores are put in the order of their names (code point order) and given out one unit at a time. Each goes to the person who comes first in the tie order and for whom a split with the smallest total cost is still possible.

Several splits have the same smallest total cost this time. The tie was settled by the order drawn from the public random source in the rules. Seed (SHA-256): 922852d168b3779a289d51e88d895c7256c936dbe165ce8b600eb1dbb6dbb63f. The algorithm is SEED_SPEC in fsq_core.

How to recompute the tie order: (1) seed = SHA-256 of the seed preimage (UTF-8). (2) Draw number c (counting from 0) = the first 8 bytes, big-endian, of SHA-256(seed + c as 8 big-endian bytes), read as an integer x. For an integer in 0..n-1, take x mod n if x < 2^64 - (2^64 mod n), otherwise add 1 to c and draw again. (3) Sort the names by code point, go i from n-1 down to 1, draw an integer j in 0..i and swap positions i and j. The result is the tie order. The random module is never used.

One line per person (send it to that person only, not to the group):
- 大刘: By your own costs, your share comes to -45.00 a week, and no other share (with the payment that goes with it) is a better deal for you. The share of 阿明 would be exactly as good a deal for you (also -45.00), and a tie is not a loss.
- 小红: By your own costs, your share comes to -45.00 a week, and no other share (with the payment that goes with it) is a better deal for you.
- 阿明: By your own costs, your share comes to -45.00 a week, and no other share (with the payment that goes with it) is a better deal for you.

## Not modeled

- Whether the costs are honest: the script only does arithmetic on the numbers the people gave, and nobody sees another person's numbers, so it cannot check them.
- Strategy-proofness: this method is not strategy-proof. A person who knows what the others entered can sometimes end up better off by misreporting their costs.
- How chores relate: a person's cost is the sum over their chores. Chores that are easier or harder together, or that depend on the order, are not modeled.
- Money and cost trade one for one, there is no budget limit, and how much money a person already has does not matter.
- Tied splits: when several splits have the same total cost, the order fixed in advance picks one of them. That one is not guaranteed to be the best for the worst-off person.
- The worst-off person is as well off as possible within the one split of smallest total cost, not among all splits. A split with a higher total cost sometimes leaves the worst-off person better off.
- Across weeks: this plan settles one typical week and repeats it. It does not rotate anything between weeks.
- What an outside cleaner costs: not calculated. Use bill-split to share that.
- Tasks that somebody likes: costs cannot be negative, so enter 0 for a task that somebody likes.
